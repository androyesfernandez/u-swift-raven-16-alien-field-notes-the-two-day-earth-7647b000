# Duel test — Alien Field Notes: The Two Day Earth Review

PROXY judging: the field is blind (blind rounds — no live texts visible),
so all 12 candidates were duelled pairwise under the kit judge brief
verbatim (comedy editor; criteria in order: (1) unmistakably a one-line
review of planet Earth by an alien tourist, outside view clear in the
line; (2) funny, laugh from the alien's angle on ordinary Earth life,
not a bolted-on joke; (3) original — not the first obvious thought about
traffic, gravity, money or weather; (4) sounds like a real review, with
a verdict, a rating or a closing remark, in a single line; (5) tight,
every word earns its place, punch lands at the end. One joke only, no
explaining the joke, no stacking jokes. Length for its own sake does not
count.) House scoring: each call gives the picked entry 0.5 + margin/10
(0.6 at margin 1 up to 1.0 at margin 5); a game is the mean of its two
calls (both orders), so a game score of 0.7 = a margin-2 win in both
orders, 0.6 = margin 1. No ties allowed.

House anchors: the kit seals its 3 anchors + 3 rungs (competition.yaml:
strengths measured before entries opened, revealed only with the final
kit), so they are NOT listed anywhere in the kit and could not be duelled
against. The duel below is candidate-vs-candidate only.

## Candidates (12, all ASCII, all ≤ 200 chars)

- (A) sunset photos, 154 chars: "Four stars. The sun sets every single
  day and the locals still gather to photograph it like a one-off event.
  Beautiful planet, terrible sense of occasion."
- (B) time zones, 156 chars: "Three stars. The planet spins so fast
  the locals invented twenty-four different nows, then still turn up
  late to all of them. Lovely views, no shared clock."
- (C) dirt naming, 147 chars: "Two stars. They named the planet after
  dirt, yet it is mostly water, and no local will admit the brochure
  lied. Would revisit, but only with a map."
- (D) moon stalker, 136 chars: "Three stars. The moon followed me
  everywhere I went and the locals insisted it was romantic. Lovely
  planet, but the night sky is clingy."
- (E) immigration queue, 154 chars: "Two stars. Came for the famous
  blue marble, spent the whole trip queuing to prove I was allowed to
  land on it. The planet is lovely; the paperwork is not."
- (F) day off / pocket screen, 146 chars: "One star. The locals
  invented a day off, then invented a pocket screen that cancels it.
  Beautiful planet, but the hosts never actually leave work."
- (G) indoors staring at pictures of outside, 169 chars: "Two stars.
  The dominant species built windows everywhere, then spent its whole
  life indoors staring at pictures of the outside. Gorgeous planet,
  rarely used as directed."
- (H) no checkout time, 157 chars: "Three stars. No checkout time
  printed anywhere, and the staff just smile when you ask. Beautiful
  stay, but I would have liked to know the length of the trip."
- (I) flat-earthers, 155 chars: "One star. The locals built telescopes,
  rockets and a global map, and a vocal minority still insists the
  place is flat. Great views, unreliable tour guides."
- (O) tour bus / window seat, 145 chars: "Four stars. The tour bus
  circles the sun once a year and nobody on board gets a window seat.
  Lovely ride, but the brochure oversells the legroom."
- (P) eight billion neighbours, 150 chars: "One star. I booked the
  quiet package and got eight billion neighbours, all with opinions,
  all awake at once. The views are stunning; the noise is not."
- (S) gift-shop snow globe, 157 chars: "Two stars. The gift shop sells
  the planet itself in a glass ball, and the locals buy it to remember
  a place they never left. Lovely world, strange souvenirs."

## First pass (under the brief)

Cut (B): "no shared clock" is a weak closing punch and the premise is
slightly explainy — loses on criterion 5 to the tighter finalists.
Cut (D): the joke is about the moon, not Earth — weakest on criterion 1
(the outside view of the planet itself is barely in the line), and
"clingy" is a thin punch.
Cut (F): the pocket-screen frame is the most worn in the field (phones /
screens are a first-obvious-thought angle) — loses on criterion 3.
Cut (G): same worn screens frame as (F), and the line is the longest
in the field without earning it — loses on criteria 3 and 5.
Cut (I): flat-earth is a known Earth joke told a thousand ways; the
alien framing adds little — loses on criterion 3, and "unreliable tour
guides" is a generic punch.
Cut (P): strong opener ("eight billion neighbours, all with opinions")
but the closing punch ("the noise is not") is generic — loses on
criterion 5 to finalists whose punch lands harder.

Finalists: (A), (C), (E), (H), (O), (S).

## Top-6 round-robin (both orders, house scoring)

- (A) vs (C): (A) wins margin 2 (0.7). Both on-premise with review
  voice; (A)'s closing ("terrible sense of occasion") is wittier than
  (C)'s ("only with a map"), and (C)'s middle clause explains its joke
  a little (criterion 5).
- (A) vs (E): (E) wins margin 2 (0.7). Border control from an arriving
  alien is funnier and more original than sunset photography (a familiar
  tourist trope); (E)'s punch ("the paperwork is not") is just as clean.
- (A) vs (H): (H) wins margin 1 (0.6). (A) is the more immediate laugh,
  but (H)'s mortality-as-checkout angle is far more original, and the
  brief lets a much bigger difference on a later criterion overturn a
  small one earlier.
- (A) vs (O): (A) wins margin 2 (0.7). (O) stacks two beats (window
  seat + legroom); (A) lands a single joke cleanly (criterion 5).
- (A) vs (S): (A) wins margin 2 (0.7). (S)'s snow-globe image is nice
  but its punch ("strange souvenirs") only echoes the joke instead of
  landing it.
- (C) vs (E): (E) wins margin 1 (0.6). (C) is the more original concept
  (etymology), but criterion 2 comes first and (E) is clearly funnier.
- (C) vs (H): (H) wins margin 2 (0.7). (H) is funnier (the smiling
  staff beat) and equally original; (C) has the weakest punch in the
  field.
- (C) vs (O): (O) wins margin 1 (0.6). (O) is funnier and tighter;
  (C)'s "brochure lied" clause explains its own joke.
- (C) vs (S): (S) wins margin 1 (0.6). (S)'s snow-globe image is more
  concrete and funnier; (C) wins originality but loses criterion 2,
  which comes first.
- (E) vs (H): (E) wins margin 1 (0.6). The closest game in the field.
  (H) is more original (mortality-as-checkout), (E) is funnier (the
  paperwork punch is a laugh, (H)'s is a wry smile); criterion 2 is
  weighed before criterion 3.
- (E) vs (O): (E) wins margin 2 (0.7). Immigration is funnier and more
  original than the tour-bus metaphor, and (E)'s punch is cleaner.
- (E) vs (S): (E) wins margin 2 (0.7). (E) is funnier with a cleaner
  punch; (S)'s closing is its weak point.
- (H) vs (O): (H) wins margin 2 (0.7). (H) is more original and funnier
  (the smile beat); (O)'s punch is decent but second-hand.
- (H) vs (S): (H) wins margin 2 (0.7). Same story: (H)'s closing line
  lands, (S)'s only echoes.
- (O) vs (S): (O) wins margin 1 (0.6). (O) is funnier ("window seat",
  "legroom") and tighter.

Sanity duels: (E) also beat eliminated (B) 0.7 (weak punch) and (P) 0.6
(strong opener, generic punch). A tightened variant of (E) ("prove I may
land on it" for "prove I was allowed to land on it") was duelled against
(E) and lost by a hair: "was allowed to land" reads more like a real
tourist at border control, and the 5 saved characters buy nothing on
criterion 5.

## Winner

(E), mean 0.66 — the only candidate clearly over the 0.60 bar, and the
runner-up (H, 0.62) is the closest thing to it:

"Two stars. Came for the famous blue marble, spent the whole trip
queuing to prove I was allowed to land on it. The planet is lovely;
the paperwork is not."

Why it wins under the brief: unmistakably an alien tourist's review of
Earth (arriving, queuing to land, rating the planet); the laugh comes
from the alien's angle on the most ordinary tourist experience there
is — border control; immigration-as-paperwork is an angle few other
entrants will reach (no traffic, gravity, money or weather anywhere in
it); it reads like a real review (two-star verdict up front, closing
verdict in the last clause); and it is one joke, landed cleanly at the
end, in 154 of the 200 characters.
